01 · PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS

The Tourist Acquisition Problem

관광객 확보 문제

Olive Young wins the tourist in-store. The products, the brand recognition, the purchase — all there. But the moment they leave Korea, Olive Young loses them permanently. The entire loyalty and CRM infrastructure assumes a Korean resident.

I didn't find this problem in a report. I lived it. As a Malaysian Muslim in Seoul, I walked into Olive Young and couldn't sign up for their loyalty program — no Korean phone number, no access. That single friction point is the entire business case.말레이시아 출신 무슬림으로 서울에서 직접 이 문제를 경험했습니다. 한국 번호가 없으면 멤버십 가입이 불가능합니다.

Query 1Where does the loyalty funnel break for foreign customers?

외국인 고객의 로열티 퍼널 이탈 지점

SQL · POSTGRESQL
-- Loyalty signup funnel by customer nationality
-- Identifies drop-off at each step: foreign vs domestic

SELECT
  customer_type,
  COUNT(*) AS total_transactions,
  SUM(CASE WHEN loyalty_prompted = TRUE THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS prompted,
  SUM(CASE WHEN signup_attempted = TRUE THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS attempted,
  SUM(CASE WHEN signup_completed = TRUE THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS completed,
  ROUND(
    SUM(CASE WHEN signup_completed = TRUE THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100.0
    / NULLIF(COUNT(*), 0), 1
  ) AS completion_rate_pct
FROM   transactions
WHERE  transaction_date >= '2024-01-01'
GROUP BY customer_type
ORDER BY completion_rate_pct DESC;

Query 2Which SKUs have the highest tourist purchase concentration?

관광객 구매 비중이 높은 SKU 식별

SQL · POSTGRESQL
-- Top SKUs by tourist purchase share vs domestic
-- Flags products with high tourist dependency

SELECT
  p.sku_name, p.category,
  COUNT(CASE WHEN t.customer_type != 'domestic' THEN 1 END) AS tourist_orders,
  COUNT(*) AS total_orders,
  ROUND(
    COUNT(CASE WHEN t.customer_type != 'domestic' THEN 1 END) * 100.0
    / NULLIF(COUNT(*), 0), 1
  ) AS tourist_share_pct,
  ROUND(AVG(t.basket_size_krw)) AS avg_basket_krw
FROM   transactions t
JOIN   products p ON t.sku_id = p.sku_id
GROUP BY p.sku_name, p.category
HAVING tourist_share_pct > 30
ORDER BY tourist_share_pct DESC
LIMIT 6;

Query 3How much recurring revenue is leaking annually?

연간 반복 매출 누수 규모 추정

SQL · POSTGRESQL · CTE
-- Revenue leakage: tourists not retained in CRM
-- Assumes 25% retention, 6x annual reorders at avg ₩38,000

WITH tourist_base AS (
  SELECT customer_type,
    COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) AS unique_tourists,
    SUM(revenue_krw)             AS instore_revenue
  FROM transactions
  WHERE customer_type != 'domestic'
    AND transaction_year = 2024
  GROUP BY customer_type
),
leakage AS (
  SELECT *,
    ROUND(unique_tourists * 0.25)             AS recoverable_25pct,
    ROUND(unique_tourists * 0.25 * 6 * 38000) AS est_annual_recurring_krw
  FROM tourist_base
)
SELECT *,
  ROUND(est_annual_recurring_krw / instore_revenue * 100, 1) AS recurring_vs_instore_pct
FROM leakage
ORDER BY est_annual_recurring_krw DESC;

04 · STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATION

Three Interlocking Solutions

세 가지 연동 솔루션

Each solution targets a different point in the tourist journey. Together they form a closed loop: capture data in-store → increase basket size → retain globally post-trip.

1
QR-to-global membership at checkout
계산대 QR 글로벌 멤버십 가입
Receipt QR links tourist to a global profile via passport scan, Google, or social login — no Korean number required. Creates a CRM profile that transitions to Olive Young's international e-commerce app post-return. This is the data capture layer: without it, the other solutions have no customer to retain.
2
International counter + home postage service
국제 카운터 · 해외 직배송 서비스
Dedicated in-store counter at flagship locations (Myeongdong, Hongdae, Sinchon) where tourists ship purchases home directly. Removes the luggage constraint that self-limits basket size. Dopamine shopping peaks during travel — willingness to buy more is high, but "how do I carry this?" is the blocker. Estimated +22% basket uplift based on comparable duty-free shipping data.
3
Post-trip retention loop + wishlist capture
귀국 후 재구매 유도 루프 · 위시리스트 수집
At QR scan, tourist saves a digital wishlist of browsed-but-not-purchased items. Post-trip email sequence (Day 3 / Day 14 / Day 30) targets wishlist items with international shipping enabled. Converts browse intent into delayed purchase — addressing the "add-to-cart without buying" dopamine behaviour. Expected conversion: 12–18% of wishlist captures to first international purchase within 30 days.

Declared assumptions · 선언된 가정

All assumptions are stated before analysis. Undeclared assumptions receive no analytical credit — this is declared upfront.

Base demand (Laneige Lip Mask)
18,000–23,000 units/month domestic baseline. Proxy from Olive Young top-seller category share estimates and Korean beauty market size reports. Tourist overlay adds 38% in peak months (Jul–Aug, Jan–Feb) based on KTO seasonality data.
SQL dataset scale
Simulated: ~383,000 total transactions across 4 customer types. Ratios (prompted, attempted, completed) derived from reasonable assumptions about loyalty program UX friction. Completion rate of 63.7% for domestic users aligns with industry loyalty signup benchmarks (60–70%).
Revenue leakage model
25% retention rate is conservative (industry e-commerce re-engagement benchmarks: 20–35% for travel-triggered purchases). 6 annual reorders at ₩38,000 average derived from Olive Young global average order value estimates and repurchase cycle for skincare (every 2 months).
APP strategy (Hybrid)
Hybrid chosen over Level because tourist peaks are concentrated (4–6 weeks, not spread). Overtime cost assumed at 1.5× regular labour. Holding cost assumed ₩500/unit/month. No backlog assumed — stockouts filled by safety stock drawdown then subcontracting.
+22% basket size uplift
Based on duty-free in-store shipping services (Japan, Singapore airport retail) where eliminating luggage friction increased average transaction value by 18–26%. Applied 22% as midpoint.
Shipping counter revenue (₩18B)
₩30,000 average shipping fee (competitive with EMS/K-Post international rates) × 600,000 estimated annual shipments (conservative: ~10% of total international tourist transactions opting in).

ANALYST REFLECTION

My Thinking & Skills Applied

분석 과정과 적용된 역량

Why I chose this problem
이 문제를 선택한 이유
Most portfolio projects start from a dataset. This one started from a personal frustration. When I moved to Seoul, I noticed immediately that Korean retail infrastructure wasn't built for people like me — foreign, non-Korean-speaking at first, without a local phone number. The Olive Young loyalty system was just one example of a broader pattern.

What made it interesting analytically is that this isn't a product problem or a brand problem. Olive Young's products are excellent — that's exactly why international tourists love them. It's a systems and data architecture problem. The brand wins the customer in-store and then has no mechanism to keep them. That gap between in-store success and zero post-purchase relationship is where the business case lives.
Skills I applied and what I learned
적용한 역량과 배운 점

Funnel analysis thinking: At Delivery Hero I learned to ask "where exactly does the drop-off happen?" before jumping to solutions. That same discipline shapes Query 1 here — I didn't just note that foreigners don't sign up, I traced the funnel step by step to identify which specific gate fails. This is the difference between observing a problem and diagnosing it.

SQL as a thinking tool, not just an output tool: The three queries here aren't just code — they represent three different analytical lenses: funnel analysis (Query 1), segmentation (Query 2), and financial modelling (Query 3 with CTEs). I chose CTEs for Query 3 specifically because the leakage calculation has two logical stages — tourist sizing, then revenue projection — and CTEs make that logic readable and auditable.

Demand forecasting method selection: The most important decision in forecasting isn't the calculation — it's choosing the right method for the demand pattern. Applying ARIMA to a viral-spike SKU like COSRX would produce meaningless forecasts. The method-to-pattern matching table in the Forecasting tab is the core analytical judgment, not the numbers themselves.

Business analyst documentation (user stories): Writing user stories with proper acceptance criteria is harder than it looks. "As a tourist, I want to sign up" is not a user story — it has no context, no goal, and no testable outcome. The stories in the How I Work tab are written to be implementable: a developer could read them and know exactly what to build and how to test it.

What I would do differently with more time/data: I would validate the funnel drop-off rates with real Olive Young app analytics rather than simulated proxies. I would also run A/B tests on the QR signup flow at a single store before recommending full rollout — the 25% retention assumption is reasonable but unvalidated. The shipping counter basket uplift (+22%) relies on duty-free analogues; a pilot at one Myeongdong store would give real lift data within 3 months.

My edge as an analyst for this problem
이 문제에 대한 나만의 강점
I am the customer this analysis is about. Not metaphorically — literally. I have walked into Olive Young as a foreign tourist, tried to sign up, failed, and left my data footprint nowhere. I have carried heavy shopping bags through Incheon Airport because there was no in-store shipping option. I have navigated product labels in a language I was still learning.

This gives me something most analysts don't have: a pre-built intuition for which friction points matter most to the customer. The four pain points in the Problem tab are not the result of desk research — they are the result of lived experience, structured into a business framework.
